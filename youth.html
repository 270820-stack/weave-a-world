Youth Action keep them alive

Every tradition in this collection survives only if a new generation takes it up. Gathered below are all fifty actions from the ten posters — practical ways for students and youth groups to grow dye plants, record elders' knowledge, run workshops, and carry these living colours forward. Jump to any dye, or scroll through them all.

No. 01 · Nigeria & Mali

The Living Blue: Indigo Heritage of West Africa

Full poster →
  1. Start community indigo gardens to grow dye plants.
  2. Record elders' oral histories and traditional recipes.
  3. Organise dyeing workshops and craft fairs.
  4. Develop eco-conscious fashion projects linking heritage and innovation.
  5. Use digital storytelling to raise international awareness of West African indigo.
No. 02 · Mexico & Peru

The Living Red: Cochineal in Mexico & Peru

Full poster →
  1. Start school or community cactus gardens to cultivate nopal and cochineal insects, linking biology, ecology, and traditional dyeing.
  2. Record elders' stories, myths, and recipes about cochineal and its role in clothing, rituals, and trade for digital archives and classroom use.
  3. Organise natural dye workshops, fashion circles, and local markets where cochineal-dyed textiles are demonstrated and sold alongside other sustainable crafts.
  4. Develop eco-conscious fashion and product lines — scarves, yarns, accessories — that highlight cochineal reds and share transparent stories of Indigenous makers.
  5. Use podcasts, short films, and social media campaigns to explain how a small insect on a cactus transformed global history and why protecting cochineal traditions matters today.
No. 03 · India

The Living Red: Madder Root in India

Full poster →
  1. Start school herb gardens that include madder and other dye plants, documenting their growth, harvesting, and dye properties.
  2. Record elders' stories about red saris, wedding turbans, and ritual cloth, noting where natural dyes like madder once played a central role.
  3. Organise natural dye clubs and workshops where students experiment with madder on small fabric samples and create collaborative art or fashion pieces.
  4. Launch youth-led, eco-conscious fashion micro-brands that clearly label and celebrate madder-dyed items, connecting customers to farmer and dyer stories.
  5. Use zines, social media, and short videos to teach peers how madder links chemistry, ecology, and the cultural meanings of red in Indian life.
No. 04 · Britain, Ireland & Gaul

The Living Blue: Woad in Celtic Europe

Full poster →
  1. Start community dye gardens with woad and other native plants, documenting their growth and blue-yielding processes.
  2. Record local legends and folklore about “blue warriors” and woad's role in ancient Celtic life.
  3. Organise workshops where students produce woad dye vats, experiment with natural fabrics, and display their results as modern art or fashion.
  4. Launch youth-led eco-craft brands that celebrate woad's revival story, connecting farmers, dyers, and young creators.
  5. Use social media and short storytelling videos to explore how woad connects chemistry, ecology, and cultural identity — bringing Europe's ancient blue back to life.
No. 05 · United States (Diné)

The Living Gold: Marigold in Navajo Traditions

Full poster →
  1. Grow marigolds in community and school gardens to learn dye extraction from local ecology.
  2. Collect elders' stories about rugs and blankets coloured with natural yellows and their meanings.
  3. Host workshops combining botany, chemistry, and traditional weaving using marigold dye.
  4. Develop youth-brand projects that label and celebrate marigold-dyed eco-textiles, connecting consumers to Navajo artistry.
  5. Use zines, short videos, and social posts to teach peers how marigold embodies the bond between sunlight, earth, and cultural identity.
No. 06 · Iran

The Living Gold: Saffron in Persia (Iran)

Full poster →
  1. Cultivate saffron in school or community gardens to learn its growth cycle and heritage value.
  2. Document stories and customs related to saffron use in Persian celebrations such as Nowruz.
  3. Host workshops combining chemistry, culture, and dye experiments with saffron.
  4. Create student-led sustainable brands that showcase saffron-dyed garments linked to Iranian craft traditions.
  5. Use short videos and digital exhibits to teach how saffron bridges science, sunlight, and cultural symbolism in Persian life.
No. 07 · Japan

The Living Blue: Shibori Indigo in Japan

Full poster →
  1. Host pattern-making workshops where students learn basic shibori binding, folding, and clamping techniques.
  2. Compare shibori with other tie-dye forms — such as Indian bandhani or West African adire — and document the similarities and differences.
  3. Visit or virtually tour indigo studios and the Arimatsu festival, recording interviews with artisans for school archives.
  4. Grow Japanese indigo in classroom gardens and experiment with fresh-leaf dyeing to observe the chemistry first-hand.
  5. Share finished shibori pieces and process videos online to introduce 'Japan blue' to international audiences.
No. 08 · Finland & Karelia

The Living Amber: Birch Bark in Finland

Full poster →
  1. Identify local dye plants — birch, alder, heather, and others — and map where they grow near school or home.
  2. Collaborate on eco-friendly dyeing projects using responsibly gathered bark and leaves, documenting each recipe and result.
  3. Interview grandparents and local craftspeople about folk costume, recording which colours and plants their families used.
  4. Create a shared swatch archive comparing birch yellows and tans across seasons, mordants, and water sources.
  5. Present findings at local festivals or online, linking Finnish forest chemistry to global natural-dye heritage.
No. 09 · Morocco to Rajasthan

The Living Earth: Henna in North Africa & India

Full poster →
  1. Exchange henna designs across cultures — comparing Moroccan geometric, Indian floral, and Gulf styles — and document their meanings.
  2. Record family celebrations where henna appears, building an archive of photographs, stories, and regional recipes.
  3. Try fabric block-printing and wool dyeing with natural henna to explore its chemistry beyond body art.
  4. Learn to identify pure henna and raise awareness about the dangers of chemical 'black henna' additives.
  5. Host intercultural Mehndi evenings at school or community centres, inviting elders and artists to teach traditional motifs.
No. 10 · Jamaica, Belize & Campeche

The Living Shadow: Logwood in the Caribbean

Full poster →
  1. Research local dye trees and the history of the logwood trade in your parish or district, mapping old logwood stations and place names.
  2. Produce logwood swatch collections showing how mordants and pH change the colour, and share the results with other schools.
  3. Create history projects linking logwood to Caribbean stories of piracy, colonial trade, maroon resistance, and emancipation.
  4. Interview beekeepers, dyers, and elders about logwood's place in local livelihoods, past and present.
  5. Exhibit logwood-dyed craft alongside its history at school fairs and cultural festivals, reclaiming the dye's Caribbean legacy.
